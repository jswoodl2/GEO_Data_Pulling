Spina bifida (SB) can associate with reduced fetal growth. However, little is known about placental development and function in pregnancies with fetal SB, despite that the placenta is a critical regulator of fetal growth. WHAT QUESTION DID THIS STUDY ADDRESS? How is placental gene expression altered in fetuses with SB? Is dysregulation in placental genes and networks evident in gene sets that are nutrient‐sensitive, or those critical for fetoplacental growth? WHAT DOES THIS STUDY ADD TO OUR KNOWLEDGE? Fetuses with SB have dysregulation in placental genes not previously known to associate with SB, and in placental genes that have known links to SB but have not been previously identified in the placenta. Fundamental placental processes are also dysregulated in fetuses with SB, including gene sets involved in placental nutrient transport, branching angiogenesis, and immune/inflammatory processes. Several dysregulated placental genes and gene regulatory networks in fetuses with SB are sensitive to multiple micronutrients, emphasizing the need to expand beyond a folic acid‐centric view to understand SB and its comorbidities. This study is the first to assess global placental gene expression in fetuses with SB and the first to use a novel nutrient‐focused gene expression analysis pipeline in gestational tissues. It is also among the first to interrogate placental phenotype in fetuses with SB more broadly. HOW MIGHT THIS CHANGE CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY OR TRANSLATIONAL SCIENCE? An improved understanding of placental phenotype in fetuses with SB may help identify novel mechanisms associated with comorbidities in fetuses with SB and reveal new therapeutic targets to improve fetal outcomes in this population. INTRODUCTION Risk factors for neural tube defects (NTDs) include nutritional factors, 1 2 3 4 5 The risk factors known to associate with NTDs are likely to also disrupt proper placental growth and development, which may in part explain why fetuses with NTDs have an increased risk of growth restriction and early birth. 6 7 8 9 10 6 7 8 7 To better understand how placental function is altered in fetuses with NTDs, we used data from a case–control study to determine how the placental transcriptome differs in fetuses with isolated spina bifida (SB; the most common NTD; cases), compared to fetuses without any congenital anomalies (controls). We hypothesized that placental transcriptome profiles in cases would be distinct from controls, especially in cases with poorer growth outcomes, and that dysregulation would be evident in gene sets critical for fetoplacental growth. We also hypothesized that multiple nutrient‐sensitive gene networks would be dysregulated in cases. Fetuses with structural anomalies outside the SB spectrum and those with genetic anomalies were excluded. Pregnant people carrying a fetus without congenital anomalies were recruited from our low‐risk pregnancy unit (controls) around 25 weeks' gestation. Placentae were collected from 12 cases and 10 controls. Our work uncovered four major themes related to placental dysfunction in fetuses with isolated SB, advancing our understanding of SB phenotypes and placental mechanisms that may contribute to comorbidities in affected fetuses. First, we found dysregulation in gene networks that have known links to NTDs, but which have not been previously profiled in the placenta. Second, we identified new signatures in fundamental placental processes, including placental transport and growth, that were dysregulated in cases. Next, we found that several of the dysregulated placental gene networks in cases were sensitive to multiple nutrients, and that mothers of cases reported dietary intakes with lower nutritional value and higher inflammatory potential. These findings support a need to expand beyond a folic acid‐centric view to understand NTD phenotypes and comorbidities. Last, we found dysregulation in gene networks related to inflammation and immune regulation in cases. Collectively, our findings suggest that the placental transcriptome is altered in fetuses with SB, and that placental dysfunction in fetuses with SB may, in part, underlie poor fetal growth, and contribute to subsequent comorbidities 30 31 We have shown that several genes and gene sets with known links to NTDs are dysregulated in placentae from fetuses with isolated SB. The COPII pathway was downregulated in case placentae and is a critical regulator of forward trafficking of proteins and lipids from the ER. 32 33 34 35 CAT CAT 36 CAT 37 Cases also had dysregulation in placental gene networks related to placental growth, which could contribute to placental dysfunction and common SB‐associated comorbidities, such as poor fetal growth. Half of the cases were also delivered following pregnancy termination, however, as terminations occurred at an earlier GA than the liveborn cases delivered, it is difficult to delineate the potential effects of the termination procedure versus GA on placental gene expression. This is a limitation of our study, and a larger cohort would be needed to evaluate the effects of termination or liveborn with fetoscopic repair on placental phenotype in fetuses with SB. Further, whereas the inclusion of an additional control group (PT controls) that was GA‐matched to cases is a strength of this study, placental dysfunction is associated with PT birth, and it is possible that the PT controls themselves may have had placental maldevelopment, which could have confounded our comparisons. However, in an aim to address this limitation, we took a conservative approach when identifying the DEG list, and only considered the overlap between the two comparison lists: cases compared to controls (who were born full term) and cases compared to PT controls. Our findings of dysregulated gene networks known to associate with SB pathogenesis suggests functional perturbations contributing to SB may also present in placentae, which could contribute to placental dysfunction. Studying placental maldevelopment and dysfunction, including nutrient‐gene interactions, in fetuses with isolated SB may help identify novel mechanisms associated with comorbidities in fetuses with SB and new targets for improving fetal outcomes.
