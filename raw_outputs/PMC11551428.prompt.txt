Here, we have used single-nucleus RNA-seq analysis to determine how exposure to pharmacologically relevant levels of opioid drug oxycodone (OXY) affects different mouse placenta cell lineages. These placental cells include parietal trophoblast giant cells (pTGC), large secretory cells that participate in controlling maternal metabolism to accommodate the pregnancy, as well in the placenta–brain axis of the developing fetus. The pTGC have eluded previous attempts to characterize their transcriptome profile beyond the initial stages of pregnancy. OXY exposure caused extensive loss of pTGC and transcriptomic changes indicative of stress throughout the junctional zone of the placenta that borders maternal uterine tissue. Pregnant women are often prescribed or abuse opioid drugs. The placenta is likely the key to understanding how opioids cause adverse pregnancy outcomes. Maternal oxycodone (OXY) exposure of pregnant mice leads to disturbances in the layer of invasive parietal trophoblast giant cells (pTGC) that forms the interface between the placenta and uterus. These cells are analogous to extravillous trophoblasts of the human placenta. They are crucial to coordinating the metabolic needs of the conceptus with those of the mother and are primary participants in the placenta–brain axis. Their large nuclear size, however, has precluded both single-cell (sc) and single-nucleus (sn) RNA-seq analyses beyond embryonic day (E) 8.5. Here, we compared the transcriptomes of placentas from pregnant mice exposed to OXY with unexposed controls at E12.5, with particular emphasis on the pTGC. The nonfluidic Parse snRNA-seq approach permitted characterization of the nuclear transcriptomes of all the major placental cell lineages and their presumed progenitors at E12.5. OXY exposure had a negligible effect on components of the placental labyrinth, including the two syncytial cell layers, but caused transcriptomic changes consistent with metabolic stress throughout the spongiotrophoblast. Most notably, there was loss of the majority of pTGC, whose normal gene expression is consistent with elevated energy demand relating to biosynthesis of multiple secretory products, especially hormones, and endoduplication of DNA.
