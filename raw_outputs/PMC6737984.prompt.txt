The trophoblast-specific PcG protein SFMBT2 is known to be required for maintenance of trophoblast progenitors. Rather than binding to trophoblast-specific genes repressed in TSC, SFMBT2 is concentrated at chromocentres and regions rich in repetitive elements, specifically LINE sequences and major satellites, suggesting that it is involved in higher-order organization of the trophoblast genome. It is also found enriched at a subset of ncRNAs. Comparison of ChIP-seq datasets for other chromatin proteins reveals several stereotypical distribution patterns, suggesting that SFMBT2 interacts with several different types of chromatin complexes specific to the trophoblast lineage. DISCUSSION Progenitor cells allow a tissue to bulk up during development or repair itself following injury. Mammals are particularly dependent on stem/progenitor cells during development because embryogenesis is accompanied by an increase in mass, unlike, for example, insect or amphibian embryos. The variable size of different mammalian species may reflect, at least in part, the renewal capacity of their stem/progenitor cell populations. The placenta, a highly specialized organ, is dependent, at least in rodents, on the integrity of the trophoblast progenitor population. Reduced numbers of TSC in embryos leads to a reduced placenta and embryonic death.
