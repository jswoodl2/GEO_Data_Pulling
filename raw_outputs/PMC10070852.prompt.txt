Conclusion We identified an environmentally challenged population (high-altitude) in which ancestry dependent natural selection may induce altered DNA methylation status, thereby benefitting placental function. In the gene dysferlin, we found a variant (rs10166384) located within a transcription factor-binding site that can alter dysferlin gene expression. Those individuals with the A allele who reside at high-altitude are likely to have decreased DNA methylation and higher dysferlin gene expression. The higher dysferlin gene expression is likely to contribute to more effective syncytiotrophoblast turnover and repair in response to the high-altitude environment, which may lead to more successful pregnancies at high-altitude. Those with the G allele are more likely to have DNA methylation along the transcription factor-binding site and decreased gene expression. This may be advantageous among those of Andean ancestry because it may allow them a way to easily modify gene expression of those genes regulated by the TEAD4 transcription factor as they move between high and low-altitude.
